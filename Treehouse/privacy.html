Privacy Policy

Treehouse · Last updated 15 October 2026

Treehouse ("the App") is developed by Ivan Cvetanović ("I", "me"). This policy explains what information the App handles and who else receives it. The App has no user accounts, and I do not run any servers that receive your data. It covers the Android version of the App.

Summary

  • Your family tree, photos and voice notes are stored on your phone. The App does not upload them, and I never receive them.
  • When you enter a person's birth place, the place name is sent to your phone's place-search service and, if that finds nothing, to OpenStreetMap, so the person can be shown on the map. The Map view downloads its map images from OpenStreetMap.
  • The free version shows rewarded video ads through Google AdMob, which may use your advertising ID (a resettable number Android gives your device for ads).
  • Purchases are handled by Google Play. I never see your payment details.
  • Android's Auto Backup is turned off for the App, so Google does not back up the App's data to your Google account. To keep or move your tree, make a backup file yourself in the App (Export > Full backup). Files you export or share go only where you send them.
  • The App does not ask for permission to use your location, contacts, camera or photo library. It asks for the microphone only when you record a voice note. The App has no analytics or crash-reporting service of its own.

Your Family Tree on Your Device

The App stores your tree in its own private storage on your phone: names, maiden names, gender, birth and death dates and places, the map coordinates found for a birth place, biographies, notes, relationships (including marriage dates and places), events, and the photos and voice notes you add. It also stores your settings, such as your theme and date format, the display name shown on files you share, which free rewards you have unlocked, and whether Premium is unlocked. Your tree may include information about other people, including living relatives and children. It stays on your phone unless you send it somewhere, as described below.

Photos are chosen with Android's picker, and the App copies only the pictures you pick into its own storage; your originals are not changed, and the App does not ask for permission to browse your photo library. Photos are copied unchanged, so any information stored inside a photo file (for example when and where it was taken, if your camera saved that) goes with the photo when you back up or send the photo files. Voice notes are recorded only when you start a recording, after Android has asked you for the microphone permission, and are saved as audio files in the App's storage. The App does not use your camera.

You can edit or delete any person, photo or voice note in the App. Deleting a person or a photo removes its files from the App's storage. Deleting a whole tree removes its people and records, but the photo and voice files that belonged to it stay in the App's private storage (they are no longer shown or exported) until you clear the App's storage or uninstall the App, which deletes them from your phone. Backup copies made by earlier versions of the App in your Google account (see the Backup section below) and files you exported or shared are not deleted by that.

Looking Up Places

When you enter a person's birth place, the App looks the place up so it can put the person on the map. The lookup runs when you leave the Birth Location field or save the person, and again when you later edit a person's birth place. It does not run on every keystroke. If the phone is offline when a lookup runs, the App remembers which person it was (not the place) and tries once more the next time you open that person. Only birth places are looked up: death places, marriage places and event places are saved as text and are not looked up. If you leave the birth place empty, nothing is sent.

The text you typed is first handed to your phone's own place-search service (Android's geocoder). On most Android phones that service is provided by Google, but your phone decides, and I do not control it. If it finds no match, or is not available, the App sends the same text to Nominatim, the search service of OpenStreetMap, at nominatim.openstreetmap.org. For family history this is not unusual, because old villages, former borders and non-Latin spellings are often not found by the first service.

The request to Nominatim is sent over HTTPS and contains the place text and the App's name and package ID ("Treehouse/1.0 (com.cvetanovic.treehouse)"). Nominatim also receives your IP address, as any website does. No names, dates, photos or other details about the person are sent. The coordinates that come back are saved with the person on your phone.

There is currently no setting to turn the lookup off. To avoid it, leave the birth place empty; the person then does not appear on the map. OpenStreetMap's privacy policy is at https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Privacy_Policy

Maps (OpenStreetMap)

The Map view shows your family's birth places on a map. The App downloads the map's pictures (tiles) from tile.openstreetmap.org over HTTPS, only when you open a map that has at least one person on it. Each request tells OpenStreetMap your IP address, which part of the world you are looking at, and the App's package name (com.cvetanovic.treehouse). It carries no names, dates or other contents of your tree. Because the map opens at the places you recorded, OpenStreetMap can see which regions you look at, but not whose they are.

The App never reads your phone's location. It has no location permission, and every pin on the map comes from a place name you typed or from coordinates that were already inside a file you opened.

Sharing and Exporting

The App can create files from your tree. They leave your phone only when you choose a destination in Android's share sheet or save dialog, and then they go wherever you send them: another app, a cloud folder, or another person. I do not receive a copy.

  • GEDCOM export: the people in your tree with their names, gender, dates, places and family links (marriage dates included).
  • PDF report and picture of your tree: names, dates, places and profile photos, as drawn in the report or on the chart.
  • Full backup: one ZIP file with every tree on the device, including every photo, voice recording and event photo, the trees' collaboration and activity records, plus your display name and your theme and date-format settings. (The App can still read older JSON backups, but no longer creates them.)
  • Sharing a tree, handing someone an editing turn, or handing over ownership: a file with the tree's people, relationships, events, places and coordinates, biographies and notes, and the photos and voice notes (when you hand someone an editing turn, you can untick the option to leave them out). It also carries your display name and the name you typed for the person it is for. The file goes directly to the person you send it to; there is no server in between.
  • Sharing one person's profile as text: their name (and maiden name), gender, birth and death dates and places, the names of their parents and partners, how many children they have, and their biography and notes.

Once you have sent a file, I cannot control what happens to it. Treat a full backup the way you would treat the family photo album itself. While a file is being sent, a temporary copy is kept in the App's private cache. The App removes older share files the next time you share something, and when it next starts if they are over an hour old; Android also clears that cache from time to time, and you can clear it yourself in the App's storage settings.

Advertising (Google AdMob)

The free version of the App shows rewarded video ads through Google AdMob. An ad is shown only after you tap the "Watch an ad" (or "Watch Ad") button: to add more people once a tree reaches the free limit, to unlock a visual theme, or to export a GEDCOM file, a PDF or a picture of your tree. There are no banners, no pop-up ads and no ads when you open the App.

Google and its advertising partners may use information such as your device information, advertising ID, IP address (from which approximate location may be inferred), diagnostic information about the ad software, and how you use the App and interact with ads (such as app launches, taps and video views) to show, measure and personalize ads, for analytics, and to prevent fraud. The App's manifest includes Android's advertising ID permission (added by Google's ad software), and Google's ad software may also use Android's Privacy Sandbox ad permissions on phones that support them. The ad request the App sends contains no keywords or other content from your tree, so no name, date, place, photo or recording from your tree is passed to the advertising system.

To have an ad ready, the free version loads a rewarded ad in the background, but only once Google's consent tool says ads may be requested (see below), and it starts Google's ad software only then. When you finish or close an ad, the App loads the next one, and if a load fails (for example because no ad is available) it tries again after a delay of a few seconds that grows, and gives up after five failures in a row; it tries again when you tap "Watch an ad" or come back to the App. This means Google may receive several ad requests when you use the App, even if you never watch an ad.

In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, Switzerland and other regions where consent is required, the App shows Google's consent form (Google's User Messaging Platform) when it starts. The advertising partners that may receive data are listed in that form. The App starts Google's ad software, and asks it for an ad, only once Google's consent tool says ads may be requested, and it checks again before every request: when the App opens, after you close an ad, when a failed request is retried, when you tap "Watch an ad" while no ad is ready, and after you change your choice. If you tap "Watch an ad" while no ad is ready, the App tells you no ad is available, and if the consent tool does not yet allow requests it offers the consent form again where one applies. Google's ad library also contains a small start-up component that Android runs when the App opens; the App does not use it to request ads, and what it does before the App has asked for anything is decided by Google.

If the consent form applies to you, you can reopen it and change your choice under Menu > Ad privacy settings in the App. That menu entry appears only when Google's consent tool reports that a way to change the choice is required. If you withdraw consent there, the App stops requesting ads straight away and discards any ad it had loaded. You can also reset your advertising ID or opt out of ad personalization in your Android settings (usually under Settings > Google > Ads; the exact path depends on your device).

Buying Premium turns ads off. Once the App knows Premium is unlocked, it does not show the consent form when it starts, does not request or show ads, and discards any ad it had already loaded. If you reinstall the App or move to a new phone, use Restore Purchases in the menu to unlock Premium again. People who have bought Premium normally do not see the consent form. More information about how Google uses data in ads and in apps that use its services:

  • https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites
  • https://policies.google.com/technologies/ads

Purchases (Google Play Billing)

The App offers an optional one-time Premium upgrade. It removes ads and the free limit on people, and unlocks all themes. It is bought through Google Play, which processes the payment. I do not receive or store your card or payment details, and the App only remembers, on your phone, whether Premium has been unlocked. When the App starts, it connects to Google Play's billing service on your phone so that it is ready for purchases, even if you never buy anything. It asks Google Play about the purchases made with your Google account only when you buy or tap Restore Purchases. Google Play may show me purchase records, such as an order ID, in the Play Console. For more information, see https://policies.google.com/privacy

Backup

The App turns Android's Auto Backup off, so Android does not back up the App's data to your Google account and does not copy it to a new phone when you set one up. I do not have access to your data either way. To keep your trees safe or move them to a new phone, make a backup file yourself (Export > Full backup, described under Sharing and Exporting) and open it on the new phone with Import > Restore a backup. Earlier versions of the App (before version 1.0.0, build 5) allowed Auto Backup, so a backup copy they made can still be held in your Google account, can be restored if you reinstall, and is not removed automatically when you uninstall the App; you can manage or delete it in your Android or Google backup settings.

Other Apps and Devices

A file you share or export can be read by the app you send it to, under that app's own privacy policy. The App can also be chosen to open a Treehouse sharing file from another app. It then copies that file (up to 100 MB) into its private cache, asks you before it adds anything (it shows the tree's name, how many people it holds and who sent it, when the file says), reads the file on your phone and deletes the copy straight after; nothing is uploaded. A Treehouse file you accept this way can add or update a tree on your phone. The copies Android's file picker makes when you choose a backup, share or GEDCOM file yourself are cleared once the App has read them. The App does not make your data available to other apps in any other way.

Children's Privacy

The App is intended for adults and is not directed at children. I do not knowingly collect personal information from children. A family tree can contain information about children in your own family. You enter it yourself and it stays on your phone unless you share it, save it in a backup file, or type a child's birth place, which is looked up as described under Looking Up Places. None of it is sent to me.

Your Choices and Rights

I do not run a server or user accounts that receive your data. Google (AdMob, Google Play and, on most phones, the phone's place-search service) and OpenStreetMap receive data as described above. You can delete the data the App stores on your phone at any time by deleting people or photos in the App (deleting a tree leaves its photo and voice files until you clear the App's storage), or by clearing its storage or uninstalling it. Your tree data stays on your phone until you do one of these. Backup copies in your Google account and files you exported or shared stay where they are until you delete them there. Copies held by Google (backup, ad data) and by OpenStreetMap follow their own policies and tools.

If you are in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom: personalized advertising is based on your consent, which you can withdraw at any time as described in the Advertising section above; the place lookup and the map images are requested only to put the birth places you typed on the map. Data may be processed by Google and the OpenStreetMap Foundation outside your country. You have the right to ask for access to, correction of, or deletion of personal information about you, to object to or restrict its use, and to complain to your local data protection authority (in the United Kingdom, the Information Commissioner's Office). Apart from the purchase records that Google Play shows me in the Play Console (such as an order ID), I hold no personal information about you, so requests about data held by Google or OpenStreetMap usually need to go to them; you can also contact me with any question at the address below.

Data Security

I take reasonable measures to protect the App and make sure it operates securely. The App's own network requests (map images and the Nominatim place search) are sent over HTTPS, and the App does not enable unencrypted (HTTP) connections. Google says it encrypts the data its ad software sends. The App does not add its own encryption to the tree stored on your phone; it relies on your phone's screen lock and Android's storage protection. However, no method of electronic storage or transmission over the internet is completely secure.
