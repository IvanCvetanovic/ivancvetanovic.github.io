Privacy Policy

Polyglot Improve · Last updated 16 October 2026

Polyglot Improve ("the App") is developed by Ivan Cvetanović ("I", "me"). This policy explains what information the App handles and who else receives it. The App has no user accounts, and I do not run any servers that receive your data.

Summary

  • Your word library, review history and settings are stored on your phone. I do not receive them.
  • Auto-Translate is the only feature where the App's own code contacts another server. When you tap it, the App sends the word you typed and your two chosen languages to Google Translate.
  • The microphone button uses your phone's own speech recognition, which may process your voice online. The App does not record or keep the audio.
  • The free version shows an occasional full-screen ad through Google AdMob, which may use your advertising ID, your IP address and how you interact with ads. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, Google's consent form asks for your choice before an ad is requested.
  • Removing ads is a one-time purchase handled by Google Play. I never see your payment details.
  • The App has no analytics or tracking tools of its own and no automatic crash reporting. It never sends its diagnostics log of app errors anywhere itself; only you can share it.
  • If Android backup is on, Google may back up the App's data to your Google account. The diagnostics log of app errors is not part of that backup.
  • The App does not ask for permission to use your location, contacts, camera or photos.

Your Words and Progress on Your Device

The App stores the following on your phone, in its own private storage: the words you study, with their translations, example sentences, readings, notes and tags; your review schedule and answer history; your training sessions, streaks, XP and achievements; the results of cross-language checks; and your settings, such as theme, session length, sounds and reminder time. It also keeps whether you bought "Remove ads" and a few counters that keep ads rare (how many sessions you have completed, the day of the first one and when an ad last appeared). Google's consent tool saves your ad-consent choice in the App's private storage too. Your library is filled from word lists bundled inside the App and from words you add or import yourself, so none of that needs an internet connection.

The App also keeps a diagnostics log: a file of up to about 200 KB with the time, the message and the technical trace of errors the App ran into. An error message can occasionally contain a word from your library. The App does not send the log anywhere. It leaves your phone only if you tap Share diagnostics under About in Settings and choose where to send it. The bin icon next to it clears the log. The log is not included in Android backup (see Backup below).

You choose what to export. Export backup in Settings packs your library, review history and progress into a .json file, and the export button on the Stats screen turns your words into CSV text. In both cases Android's share sheet opens and the data goes only to the app you pick. If you pick a cloud or messaging app, that provider will hold what you send. To hand the backup file to the share sheet, the App writes copies of it into its private cache folder. The App does not delete them itself, so they stay until Android frees cache space or you clear the App's cache in Android settings. Restore and Import from CSV read a file you choose with Android's file picker, which also leaves a copy of that file in the App's cache, kept the same way. These cache copies are not part of the Android backup.

To delete data, delete a word on its detail screen, clear the diagnostics log, or remove everything from your phone by clearing the App's storage or uninstalling it in Android settings. Copies in your Android backup are separate (see Backup below).

Auto-Translate (Google Translate)

On the Add Word screen, the translate button next to Target Word asks Google Translate for a suggestion. When you tap it, the App sends a request over HTTPS to Google's translation service (translate.googleapis.com). The request contains the word in the Source Word field and the language codes of the source and target languages you picked. The App adds no account, device or advertising identifier, and the request is made without an account or API key of mine. Besides the word and the two language codes, Google receives your IP address and the name of the software making the request (a generic Dart network identifier).

Nothing is sent unless you tap the button. The button is switched off while your phone reports no network connection, and typing the translation yourself never contacts anyone. The suggestion is placed in the Target Word field and is saved only if you save the word.

There is no setting to turn Auto-Translate off, so do not tap the translate button if you do not want a word sent. Google is an independent third party. I do not control it and cannot say how long it keeps request data. Google's handling of the request is covered by its privacy policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy

Advertising (Google AdMob)

Polyglot Improve is free because it shows an occasional full-screen ad from Google AdMob. An ad can appear when you tap Check for More on the Session Complete screen: at most one ad after every third completed session, never within 20 minutes of the previous ad, and never on the day of your first completed session or on the day after. The button reads "Ad starting…" just before an ad appears.

To have the ad ready, Google's ad library contacts Google's servers in the background while you study a session that would end with an ad. It makes no ad request during other sessions. Google's library also reports when an ad was shown or tapped. If such a report cannot be sent because you are offline, the library may retry it in the background once a connection returns, even if the app is closed.

According to Google, its ad library collects your IP address (which can indicate your general location), how you interact with ads and the app, such as app launches, taps and video views, diagnostic information, and device and account identifiers such as the Android advertising ID and an app set ID, for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention. Google and its advertising partners may use this information to show, measure and personalise ads and to prevent fraud. Polyglot Improve never sees that data and sends Google nothing about your words: the ad request contains no word, language or study information and no keywords.

You can reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of ad personalisation, in your Android settings (usually under Settings > Google > Ads; the exact path depends on your device).

More information about how Google uses data in ads and in apps that use its services:

  • https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites
  • https://policies.google.com/technologies/ads

Your Ad Choices (EEA, UK and Switzerland)

Google's consent form (its User Messaging Platform) is shown where Google requires it, such as in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland. Where it is shown, it asks for your choice before an ad is requested, and the advertising partners that may receive data are listed in it.

The App does not contact Google's consent service on the day of your first completed session or on the day after. From the second day after that first session's day, and unless you have removed ads, it asks Google's consent service whether a consent form is needed. It does this for every user, wherever you are, because Google decides from the request where you are. The check can run when you start a session that would end with an ad, when you tap Check for More (once each time the app starts, or whenever an ad is due), and when you open Settings.

You can reopen the form and change your choice at any time under Settings → Ad privacy choices. That row appears only where Google requires it, only after those first two days, and not once you have removed ads. If you do not consent to personalised ads, Google may still show ads that are not personalised.

Remove Ads (Google Play Billing)

Remove ads is a one-time purchase handled entirely by Google Play. Google, not Polyglot Improve, receives your payment details; I do not receive or store them. Google Play may show me purchase records, such as an order ID, in the Play Console. The App only learns from Google Play, on your device, that you own it, and keeps a yes/no flag.

Each time the app starts, Polyglot Improve asks Google Play (through Google's billing library and the Play Store app on your phone) which purchases your Google account owns. That is how a purchase made on another phone, or finished while the app was closed, is found. When you open Settings, it also asks Google Play what Remove ads costs in your country. Google's billing library may also report technical information about the purchase to Google. You can also use Settings → Restore purchase.

Once you own it, the App stops requesting ads and no longer asks Google for ad consent. For more information, see https://policies.google.com/privacy

Speaking and Hearing Words

  • Speaking an answer. The microphone button in a training session hands the listening to the speech-recognition service installed on your phone, set to the language of the card. On many Android phones that service is Google's and, depending on your device and its settings, it may send the audio to Google's servers to turn it into text. The App receives only the recognised text and puts it in the answer box. It does not record or keep the audio, and nothing is listened to unless you tap the microphone.
  • Hearing a word. The speaker buttons read a word or sentence aloud with your phone's text-to-speech engine, in the language of that word. The App passes the text to the engine on your phone. Depending on your device and its settings, the engine may download voices or process text online. That is handled by the engine and covered by its maker's privacy policy, not by this one.

Permissions the App Asks For

  • Internet. Used by Auto-Translate, and by Google's ad and billing libraries.
  • Network state. Lets the App see whether your phone has a connection, so it can switch off Auto-Translate and tell you when you are offline.
  • Advertising ID and Google's ad-service permissions. Declared for Google's ad library. The App's own code does not read your advertising ID; the ad library does, as described under Advertising.
  • Google Play purchases (billing). Lets Google Play's billing library check and complete the Remove ads purchase.
  • Wake lock and foreground service. Added by Google's ad library so it can retry a failed report in the background. The App declares no foreground-service type.
  • Microphone. Asked for the first time you tap the microphone button, and used only while you are speaking an answer.
  • Notifications. Asked for when you switch on reminders in Settings. They cover a daily reminder at the time you choose and a nudge when a language has gone about a week without practice. Both are scheduled on your phone, and the same switch turns both off. To time them, the App reads your phone's time zone, which stays on your phone.
  • Run at startup. Lets the reminders you set come back after your phone restarts or the App is updated.
  • Vibration. The App gives a short vibration when you answer a card, and reminder notifications can vibrate.

The App does not ask for permission to use your location, contacts, camera or photos.

Backup

The App allows Android's automatic backup. If backup is switched on for your phone, Android may copy the following to your Google account and restore it when you reinstall the App or set up a new phone: the App's database file, which holds your words, review history and training sessions, and its preferences file, which holds your streaks, XP, achievements and settings, including the reminder switch and time, the "Remove ads" flag and the ad counters. The backup rules include only those two files, so the diagnostics log and anything Google's ad and consent libraries store are not included. The same data can move in a phone-to-phone transfer. The reminder schedule itself is left out and is set up again when the App starts.

Google handles these backups under your Google account and Google's terms, and I do not have access to them. Uninstalling the App does not by itself delete backup copies in your Google account. You can switch backup off or delete them in your phone's backup settings (usually under Settings > Google > Backup; the exact path depends on your device).

Children's Privacy

The App is intended for people aged 16 and over. It is not intended for children under 16 (or under the minimum age required in your country), and I do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Your Choices and Rights

I do not run a server or user accounts that receive your data. The only personal information I hold is what you choose to send me, such as an email to the address below or a diagnostics log you share with me, and I use it only to reply to you or to find and fix a fault. Google receives data as described above: AdMob and Google Play for ads and the Remove ads purchase, Google Translate if you tap Auto-Translate and, depending on your phone, its speech-recognition, text-to-speech and backup services. You can delete the data the App stores on your device at any time, as described above. Data held by Google is governed by Google's own policies and tools.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland: personalised advertising is based on your consent, which you can withdraw at any time under Settings → Ad privacy choices; Auto-Translate and speech input happen only when you tap the button, to provide the feature you asked for. Data may be processed by Google outside your country. You have the right to ask for access to, correction of, or deletion of personal information about you, to object to or restrict its use, and to complain to your local data protection authority (in the United Kingdom, the Information Commissioner's Office). Because I hold no personal information about you unless you send it to me (apart from purchase records that Google Play shows me), requests about data held by Google usually need to go to Google, but you can contact me with any question at the address below.

Data Security

I take reasonable measures to protect the App and make sure it operates securely. The Auto-Translate request is sent over an encrypted HTTPS connection, and according to Google all data its ad library collects is encrypted in transit with TLS. On your phone, the App's data sits in its private storage, which Android keeps separate from other apps. However, no method of electronic storage or transmission over the internet is completely secure.
