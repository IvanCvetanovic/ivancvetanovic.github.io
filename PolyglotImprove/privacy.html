Privacy Policy

Polyglot Improve · Last updated 12 October 2026

Polyglot Improve ("the App") is developed by Ivan Cvetanović ("I", "me"). This policy explains what information the App handles and who else receives it. The App has no user accounts, and I do not run any servers that receive your data.

Summary

  • Your word library, review history and settings are stored on your phone. I do not receive them.
  • Auto-Translate is the only feature where the App itself contacts another server. When you tap it, the App sends the word you typed and your two chosen languages to Google Translate.
  • The microphone button uses your phone's own speech recognition, which may process your voice online. The App does not record or keep the audio.
  • The App has no ads, no purchases, no analytics or tracking tools and no automatic crash reporting, and it does not use your advertising ID. The App never sends its diagnostics log of app errors anywhere itself; only you can share it.
  • If Android backup is on, Google may back up the App's data to your Google account, including the diagnostics log of app errors.
  • The App does not ask for permission to use your location, contacts, camera or photos.

Your Words and Progress on Your Device

The App stores the following on your phone, in its own private storage: the words you study, with their translations, example sentences, readings, notes and tags; your review schedule and answer history; your training sessions, streaks, XP and achievements; the results of cross-language checks; and your settings, such as theme, session length, sounds and reminder time. Your library is filled from word lists bundled inside the App and from words you add or import yourself, so none of that needs an internet connection.

The App also keeps a diagnostics log: a file of up to about 200 KB with the time, the message and the technical trace of errors the App ran into. An error message can occasionally contain a word from your library. The App does not send the log anywhere. It leaves your phone only if you tap Share diagnostics under About in Settings and choose where to send it. The bin icon next to it clears the log. If Android backup is on, the log is included in the backup (see Backup below).

You choose what to export. Export backup in Settings packs your library, review history and progress into a .json file, and the export button on the Stats screen turns your words into CSV text. In both cases Android's share sheet opens and the data goes only to the app you pick. If you pick a cloud or messaging app, that provider will hold what you send. To hand the backup file to the share sheet, the App writes copies of it into its private cache folder. The App does not delete them itself, so they stay until Android frees cache space or you clear the App's cache in Android settings. Restore and Import from CSV read a file you choose with Android's file picker, which also leaves a copy of that file in the App's cache, kept the same way. These cache copies are not part of the Android backup.

To delete data, delete a word on its detail screen, clear the diagnostics log, or remove everything from your phone by clearing the App's storage or uninstalling it in Android settings. Copies in your Android backup are separate (see Backup below).

Auto-Translate (Google Translate)

On the Add Word screen, the translate button next to Target Word asks Google Translate for a suggestion. When you tap it, the App sends a request over HTTPS to Google's translation service (translate.googleapis.com). The request contains the word in the Source Word field and the language codes of the source and target languages you picked. The App adds no account, device or advertising identifier, and the request is made without an account or API key of mine. Besides the word and the two language codes, Google receives your IP address and the name of the software making the request (a generic Dart network identifier).

Nothing is sent unless you tap the button. The button is switched off while your phone reports no network connection, and typing the translation yourself never contacts anyone. The suggestion is placed in the Target Word field and is saved only if you save the word.

There is no setting to turn Auto-Translate off, so do not tap the translate button if you do not want a word sent. Google is an independent third party. I do not control it and cannot say how long it keeps request data. Google's handling of the request is covered by its privacy policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy

Speaking and Hearing Words

  • Speaking an answer. The microphone button in a training session hands the listening to the speech-recognition service installed on your phone, set to the language of the card. On many Android phones that service is Google's and, depending on your device and its settings, it may send the audio to Google's servers to turn it into text. The App receives only the recognised text and puts it in the answer box. It does not record or keep the audio, and nothing is listened to unless you tap the microphone.
  • Hearing a word. The speaker buttons read a word or sentence aloud with your phone's text-to-speech engine, in the language of that word. The App passes the text to the engine on your phone. Depending on your device and its settings, the engine may download voices or process text online. That is handled by the engine and covered by its maker's privacy policy, not by this one.

Permissions the App Asks For

  • Internet. Used only by Auto-Translate.
  • Network state. Lets the App see whether your phone has a connection, so it can switch off Auto-Translate and tell you when you are offline.
  • Microphone. Asked for the first time you tap the microphone button, and used only while you are speaking an answer.
  • Notifications. Asked for when you switch on reminders in Settings. They cover a daily reminder at the time you choose and a nudge when a language has gone about a week without practice. Both are scheduled on your phone, and the same switch turns both off. To time them, the App reads your phone's time zone, which stays on your phone.
  • Run at startup. Lets the reminders you set come back after your phone restarts or the App is updated.
  • Vibration. The App gives a short vibration when you answer a card, and reminder notifications can vibrate.

The App does not ask for permission to use your location, contacts, camera or photos, and it does not use your advertising ID.

Backup

The App allows Android's automatic backup. If backup is switched on for your phone, Android may copy the following to your Google account and restore it when you reinstall the App or set up a new phone: the App's database folder, which holds your words, review history and training sessions and also the diagnostics log, and its preferences file, which holds your streaks, XP, achievements and settings, including the reminder switch and time. The same data can move in a phone-to-phone transfer. The reminder schedule itself is left out and is set up again when the App starts.

Google handles these backups under your Google account and Google's terms, and I do not have access to them. Uninstalling the App does not by itself delete backup copies in your Google account. You can switch backup off or delete them in your phone's backup settings (usually under Settings > Google > Backup; the exact path depends on your device).

Children's Privacy

The App is not intended for children under 13 (or under the minimum age required in your country), and I do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Your Choices and Rights

I do not run a server or user accounts that receive your data. The only personal information I hold is what you choose to send me, such as an email to the address below or a diagnostics log you share with me, and I use it only to reply to you or to find and fix a fault. Google receives data as described above: Google Translate if you tap Auto-Translate and, depending on your phone, its speech-recognition, text-to-speech and backup services. You can delete the data the App stores on your device at any time, as described above. Data held by Google is governed by Google's own policies and tools.

If you are in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom: Auto-Translate and speech input happen only when you tap the button, to provide the feature you asked for. Data may be processed by Google outside your country. You have the right to ask for access to, correction of, or deletion of personal information about you, to object to or restrict its use, and to complain to your local data protection authority (in the United Kingdom, the Information Commissioner's Office). Because I hold no personal information about you unless you send it to me, requests about data held by Google usually need to go to Google, but you can contact me with any question at the address below.

Data Security

I take reasonable measures to protect the App and make sure it operates securely. The Auto-Translate request is sent over an encrypted HTTPS connection. On your phone, the App's data sits in its private storage, which Android keeps separate from other apps. However, no method of electronic storage or transmission over the internet is completely secure.
